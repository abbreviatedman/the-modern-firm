# What Survivors Need to Know about Filing a Sexual Assault Lawsuit

Sexual assault survivors don't just have the criminal justice system to turn to. They can also pursue civil lawsuits not just against their abusers but also against any institutions that enabled or concealed the abuse. Civil lawsuits can provide a sense of justice, as well as financial compensation. What's more, lawsuits can prevent future abuse by holding abusers and the institutions that shelter them accountable.

Survivors should know their options when it comes to sexual assault lawsuits. This includes:

- who can be sued
- what kinds of claims can be brought
- potential damages to be won
- the statute of limitations
- the general legal process involved

### Who Can Be Sued?

Many people understand that survivors can file lawsuits against the individual perpetrator of the sexual assault. But what is not as well understood is that they can also sue institutions that were negligent in preventing the abuse. This can include:

- schools
- workplaces
- religious organizations

Any entities that had a duty to protect the survivor are potentially liable for failing in that duty and allowing abuse to continue.

### What Might Come out of a Sexual Assault Lawsuit

Civil lawsuits can result in many different kinds positive outcomes for victims of sexual assault, like financial compensation for:

- medical expenses
- therapy costs
- lost wages
- pain and suffering

Additionally, successful lawsuits can lead to institutional changes that improve safety and prevent future abuse.

While none of these outcomes is guaranteed, simply bringing a civil lawsuit empowers survivors and provides closure. If successful, financial compensation can also help cover the costs associated with survivors' recovery. While monetary compensation can't undo the harm caused by sexual assault, it can provide survivors with resources to draw on to help them in their healing journey.

The legal process can be long and emotional, but survivors should know they have the right to seek justice through our civil court system.

### What Are the Statutes of Limitations on Filing a Sexual Assault Lawsuit?

Under the current statutory framework, childhood sexual abuse claims are limited by rules that allow a survivor to file civil claims within five years of discovering the psychological injury, or until they reach age 40, whichever is later.

Recently, legislators have enacted changes to allow survivors of adult sexual assault to file civil claims without a time limit. For abuse occurring on or after January 1, 2024, there is no statute of limitations at all for bringing a civil action for sexual abuse.

Additionally, for adult survivors only, there is a temporary revival window that permits plaintiffs to bring some claims that the statute of limitations would otherwise invalidate. This new window runs from January 1, 2026 through December 31, 2027 and applies to certain claims of sexual assault that occurred to a person over the age of 18.

While these changes give some opportunities for survivors to seek justice, it's important to consult with a qualified attorney to understand how the statutes of limitations may apply to your individual case.

### Evidence In Sexual Assault Lawsuits

To build a strong case, survivors should gather as much evidence as possible, including:

- medical records
- witness statements
- photographs

In addition to these examples, any communication related to the assault should be collected.

Evidence like this can help establish the facts and support the survivor's claims. While collecting evidence can be difficult, as well as emotionally triggering, it's an important step on the path to justice.

### What Practical Challenges Might Survivors Face When Filing a Sexual Assault Lawsuit?

Filing a sexual assault lawsuit can be a complex process. Survivors can face emotional hurdles, financial costs, and public scrutiny. It's important that survivors have a strong support system in place, including legal counsel, mental health professionals, and trusted friends and family members.
